## PRIMARY SOURCES: YUSUF KHAN'S EXECUTION (October 1764)

### Contemporary Newspaper/Magazine Accounts

15. **The Scots Magazine, Vol. 1765, Page 264**
    - **Type:** Primary Source - Contemporary Periodical
    - **Date:** October 1764 (published 1765)
    - **Description:** Letter from "a gentleman in the East Indies" to a friend in Scotland, dated 22 October 1764 (one week after Yusuf Khan's execution on 15 October). Written from the military camp before Palamcottah. Provides immediate eyewitness account of contemporary British perception of Yusuf Khan and reactions to his execution.
    - **Content Notes:** Describes Yusuf Khan as "descended from the ancient seed of that nation"
    - **Significance:** Valuable primary source showing how Yusuf Khan's death was perceived and reported in contemporary Britain
    - **Availability:**
      - University microfilm archives
      - Major research libraries holding Scottish periodical collections
      - Limited online digitization projects

16. **Dutch Account of Yusuf Khan's Execution**
    - **Type:** Primary Source - Foreign Correspondent Account
    - **Description:** 18th-century Dutch account of events surrounding Yusuf Khan's execution. Provides European alternative perspective on the event.
    - **Significance:** Demonstrates international awareness and documentation of Yusuf Khan's fall
    - **Availability:** Dutch colonial archives; specific location requires further research

## SUMMARY OF KEY FINDINGS ON PIGOT-YUSUF KHAN CONNECTION

### The Relationship (1755-1764)

- **Pigot's Support:** George Pigot was Yusuf Khan's "greatest supporter" during the Carnatic Wars period. Yusuf Khan had been trained in European military methods under Major Stringer Lawrence and became a highly effective commandant of the British East India Company's sepoys.

- **Peak of Yusuf Khan's Career:** By 1760, Yusuf Khan reached the height of his career as the "all-conquering" military commandant, with his guerrilla tactics repeatedly cutting French supply lines and hampering French efforts during the Carnatic Wars.

- **Pigot's Direct Encouragement:** Pigot wrote to Yusuf Khan: *"I have entrusted you with a business of great importance, not doubting but you will act therein so as to gain an advantage to the Company and the Nawab and to yourself a good name, reputation and future favour."*

### The Conflict & Fall (1764)

- **Tax Dispute:** A conflict arose when Yusuf Khan ordered all traders to render taxes directly to him, while the Nawab of Arcot wanted taxes routed through him. Governor Pigot diplomatically advised Yusuf Khan to comply with the Nawab's order.

- **The Betrayal:** Three of Yusuf Khan's associates were bribed to capture him. Yusuf Khan was executed by hanging on 15 October 1764 in Madurai. His body was dismembered and distributed—head to Trichinopoly, limbs to Tanjore, Palamcotta, and Travancore.

- **Pigot's Absence from Resolution:** By 1764, Pigot had already completed his first term as Governor (1755-1763) and had resigned in November 1763. He was not present during Yusuf Khan's final crisis and execution, though he had been instrumental in creating the circumstances of Khan's rise to power.

### Historical Significance of the Connection

The Pigot-Yusuf Khan relationship illustrates the complex dynamics of East India Company governance in the Carnatic during the 1755-1764 period:

1. **British Reliance on Local Military Talent:** Pigot's support of Yusuf Khan demonstrates how British governors depended on talented local/convertible commanders to execute military operations.

2. **Fragility of Power:** Yusuf Khan's rapid fall from favor—despite being Pigot's protégé—shows how quickly fortunes could change when different British officials with different priorities (the Nawab, the Council) gained influence.

3. **Competing Authorities:** The conflict between Pigot's authority and the Nawab of Arcot's interests created ambiguity that ultimately cost Yusuf Khan his life.

4. **Consequences of Pigot's Absence:** Pigot's second term (1775-1780) would be dominated by a similar conflict—the restoration of the Tanjore Raja—which shows his repeated patterns of challenging the established consensus within the Council.

### Primary Sources Most Relevant to Pigot-Yusuf Khan Connection

1. **Hill's "Yusuf Khan: The Rebel Commandant" (1914)** - The most comprehensive English-language treatment of their relationship

### Further Research Opportunities

1. **National Library of Scotland manuscripts** - Original correspondence of James Stuart and others involved in the 1776 coup against Pigot
2. **British Library India Office Records** - Factory records from Madras and correspondence between Madras presidency and East India Company Court of Directors
3. **Madras Record Office** - Original local documentation from the 1760s period
4. **Dutch East India Company (VOC) Archives** - Alternative European perspective on events in southern India during this period

### Geographic Context for Research

- **Madras/Chennai:** Modern city containing historical sites and some archives
- **Madurai:** City where Yusuf Khan was executed; may have local historical records
- **London:** British Library (primary India Office Records custodian)
- **Edinburgh:** National Library of Scotland (holds Stuart papers and related documents)
